about=Maps lie: every flat map must distort the globe, and the everyday web map (Mercator) inflates whatever sits far from the equator — Greenland is drawn like a continent yet Africa is about 14 times larger. 02truesize brings the much-loved thetruesize.com idea natively into QGIS and then goes further. The True Size tool lets you grab any country from the bundled offline world layer — or any polygon from your own layers — and drag it across the map; the outline is re-projected on the fly through an equal-area azimuthal projection centred under your cursor, so it always covers its true ground area. Watch Greenland deflate as it approaches the equator, rotate the shape with the R and E keys, drop as many ghost outlines as you like, and export them as a layer for printing. Size Duel is a built-in game: two countries are drawn exactly the way a web map would draw them and you guess which one is really bigger — the plugin keeps score and confesses how much the projection lied each round. The Distortion tab grades the projection you are actually using: an honesty report card with separate marks for area and shape and a plain-language verdict, classic Tissot indicatrix circles generated as a layer, and a live heads-up readout pinned to the canvas showing how stretched the map is right where you are looking. Truth Tools adds an antipode finder (click anywhere to learn where you would surface if you dug straight through the Earth — usually into the ocean), a great-circle tool that draws the genuinely shortest path next to the straight line your map suggests and reports how many kilometres the flat line wastes, and fun-unit measurements that translate any area or distance into football pitches, Central Parks, Belgiums, marathons and fractions of the way to the Moon. Everything is offline and dependency-free: a bundled generalized Natural Earth countries layer (public domain), pure-Python geodesy and native QGIS map tools. A playful, accurate companion for teaching projections, scale and spatial reasoning. Developed with feedback from educational workflows at Dokuz Eylul University, Department of City and Regional Planning.
